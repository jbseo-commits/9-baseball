# Violet Nocturne — right-handed sidearm pitcher study

This is an original adult pitcher concept and a standalone motion study. It does not replace a roster pitcher or change game balance. The character wears a midnight-plum jersey with silver piping and full-length ivory pants, throws with her right hand, and keeps the glove on her left hand. **The camera is behind home plate; every pose faces and throws toward the viewer.** An earlier left-facing sheet was rejected after review and replaced.

## Video reference leads

- [MLB: Rei Takahashi from behind the plate](https://www.mlb.com/cut4/behind-the-plate-view-of-japanese-submarine-pitcher/c-300564600): compare how a low release reads at the plate. This character's release is sidearm, higher than Takahashi's submarine slot.
- [MLB: Tyler Rogers slow-motion delivery](https://www.mlb.com/video/tyler-rogers-unique-delivery): compare stride, trunk tilt, release and follow-through. This is also lower than the intended slot.
- [MLB: Rich Hill sidearm clip](https://www.mlb.com/cut4/rich-hill-pitches-sidearm-during-nlcs-game-3-c206550496): compare the lower arm slot with ordinary delivery.

These are reference leads. The pages and descriptions were checked, but the actual moving frames were **not yet analyzed** because the available video player did not respond. The current poses follow the game's catcher-view composition requirement and are an exploratory drawing, not a video-derived timing study. No video frame is shipped or traced.

## Assets and constraints

- `source/violet-nocturne-portrait.png`: 1024×1536 transparent concept portrait.
- `source/violet-nocturne-keys.png`: 1536×1024, six catcher-facing main poses in a 3×2 transparent sheet.
- `source/violet-nocturne-bridges.png`: 1536×1024, six catcher-facing transition poses in a 3×2 transparent sheet.
- `previews/`, `atlases/` and the manifest are generated by `scripts/build-violet-nocturne-study.py`. The study uses the game's 256×256 cell, 120-frame, 60 fps, 2-second format.
- `preview.html`: a local catcher-view battle-size framing check with a frame scrubber.

The right hand, left glove, foot plant, catcher-facing release, head direction and figure scale must stay consistent. Check the authored poses and the 120-frame preview at actual game size; 120 unique output bitmaps alone do not prove smooth limb motion. The one-shot timeline ends in recovery instead of jumping back to set. Frame-mask checks still show visible jumps at stride (frame 51) and release-to-follow-through (frame 85). This is **not approved as a smooth runtime animation**.

The prototype is an art review step. Runtime integration requires video-frame study, transition redraws, and game-size motion QA. The current runtime also has side-view ball-flight assumptions in `src/duel/BallparkActors.jsx`, so a catcher-facing actor needs a coordinated camera/ball-path change rather than a simple atlas swap.
