# Pixel Umpire v2: field geometry and actor continuity plan

## Why the current scene reads as assembled parts

The scene currently mixes unrelated screen-space anchors instead of projecting one field. `groundGeometry` gets a center from the enlarged `.bp-zone`, places home plate at a fixed fraction of canvas height, moves the pitcher `21.4u` right and `78.4u` up from the zone center, then aligns the stadium field line to `pitcher.y - 12u`. The mound is separately drawn at the pitcher anchor with a fixed width of `48u`. `u` itself changes with viewport aspect ratio. Each value is internally plausible, but their relationship is not derived from a shared field or camera.

The mobile fixture makes the mismatch measurable. At a 390×484 CSS scene, the 384×477 backing canvas puts the tactical-board center near `(238,370)`, the plate near `(238,456)`, and the pitcher/rubber near `(320,176)`. That places the mound rubber about 82 pixels right of the plate centerline. The background field line is placed at y≈130 from the pitcher target. The 48u mound occupies about 184×61 canvas pixels; its centered rubber anchor at `[95,21]` makes its right edge reach x≈413, beyond the 384px canvas. The v2 batter feet fit within the actor-derived box, but that box is built along the rear-to-lead foot vector: its current corners are approximately `(57,474)`, `(199,445)`, `(195,423)`, `(52,451)`. That makes a wide, shallow, slanted rhombus instead of a fixed batter's box extending toward the pitcher. The sprite feet are useful actor anchors; they are not the field's coordinate frame.

The pitcher body target is `32u`, versus `68u` for the batter, a 0.47 near/far height ratio. The apparent near-equality comes from comparing isolated sprite crops rather than the projected player sizes against the field. The new mound and both actors therefore need one explicit depth scale and camera line, not three independent size rules. A standard field ties the pitching rubber to the back point of home plate by 60 ft 6 in, and the pitcher’s plate is elevated relative to home plate; those are useful reference constraints even though this 2D scene will use a compressed, readability-tuned perspective. See [MLB field dimensions](https://www.mlb.com/glossary/rules/field-dimensions) and the [Official Baseball Rules](https://img.mlbstatic.com/mlb-images/image/upload/mlb/wqn5ah4c3qtivwx3jatm.pdf), Rules 2.01–2.02.

The initial fixed-box revision still used the old actor offset of −4.5 ft for the box center. That is not a field dimension: it placed the box's inner edge 2.5 ft from the centerline, leaving the 17-inch plate half-width plus 21.5 inches of clearance. The MLB field diagram specifies a six-inch gap from plate edge to box and the 4 ft box width. Therefore the screen-left box center belongs at `−(17/24 + 0.5 + 2) = −3.2083 ft`; pin the rear sole 0.5 ft inside its inner boundary at `−4.7083 ft`. The batter's authored contact barrel must be checked against the projected plate edge after this coordinated shift.

## Proposed coordinate model

Represent the field in a small, fixed world coordinate system with home plate as the origin, the plate-to-rubber centerline as the depth axis, and lateral displacement as the cross-field axis. Project plate corners, the active fixed batter box, mound bounds, rubber, actor feet, actor body size, shadows, and the stadium field line through one camera function. The projection may compress the real 18.44m distance for legibility, but it must preserve these relationships:

Use one camera-depth projection for every world point, for example `screenX = cx + f * lateral / (cameraDepth + fieldDepth)` and `screenY = horizon + f * cameraHeight / (cameraDepth + fieldDepth) - f * worldHeight / (cameraDepth + fieldDepth)`. Fit `f`, `cameraDepth`, and camera pitch once per scene rectangle. Ground-plane props and actors must use that same transform; the 18.44m plate-to-rubber distance is the world-space constraint, not a screen-space offset. Keep the visible active box and plate within the scene crop by adjusting the camera fit, not by independently moving the rubber or actor.

- The pitching rubber and home-plate reference share a centerline; the current `+21.4u` lateral offset is removed unless camera yaw is explicitly represented in the world transform.
- The batter's box is a fixed world-space trapezoid, long in the plate-to-pitcher direction and wider at its near edge. Its shape does not depend on the sprite bounding box or stance foot vector. The active right-handed batter's box remains on the screen-left side of home plate; only draw the mirrored left-handed box if that field area is visible in the camera crop.
- Place the batter from its authored rear-foot anchor at one uniform actor scale, then verify the authored lead-foot anchor also lands inside the fixed box. The art's foot spacing must fit the world footprint; do not stretch the actor or slide the whole actor to chase each pitch zone.
- Derive pitcher size and mound dimensions from the same depth scale. Pin the pitcher’s authored support foot to the projected rubber. Align the mound image’s measured rubber pixel to that same point and project the mound footprint along the ground plane, rather than drawing a fixed 48u rectangle in screen space.
- Derive the stadium crop from the camera horizon used by that projection. Keep the existing home-plate/zone overlay pairing, but treat the enlarged 9-zone board as a tactical overlay rather than the physical plate or mound coordinate source.
- Use authored sole anchors for compact contact shadows. The actor ground anchors and the projected footprint must agree across ready, windup, release, and recovery poses.

This corrects placement and scale. It does not repair weak source poses by moving the stage. The current v2 atlas also has visible transition discontinuities reported by motion QA: an 84px grip jump between follow frames 8→9 and a 180px bat-tip jump between reset 13 and windup 1. The next batter packet should carry enough authored intermediate swing and return poses to make those transitions continuous. The renderer should consume optional, data-driven frame/time sequences and anchor metadata instead of assuming four frames per state. Preserve parent `impactAt`/`fxStage` timing ownership; distribute anticipation across meaningful pose holds instead of repeating a single pose, and preserve a continuous ready loop seam. Future integration should check grip, bat tip, both feet, and silhouette motion through every adjacent authored frame.

## Required geometry contract and checks

The implementation should add pure world-to-screen/project helpers with explicit camera parameters and named world points. Tests should prove that the rubber projects to the mound anchor, the plate and rubber remain aligned on the field axis, both box polygons are fixed-size in world space and become perspective trapezoids, and both ready feet remain inside the batter box at portrait, short landscape, and desktop viewports. Also test monotonic far-plane scale reduction, no actor/body or mound clipping, authored support-foot/shadow coincidence, and legacy fallback when optional v2 pose/projection metadata is absent. Browser captures should compare the 390×844 view, a low-height landscape view, and desktop; include ready, windup, contact, follow-through, whiff, and bunt.

Keep the actor source fallback and original Pixi/DOM art available until the replacement packet and screenshots pass these checks. Do not infer source-art quality from the field projection or declare the pose transition fixed by a camera adjustment.

## Risks and containment

An exact real-world projection can make a small mobile pitcher unreadable; calibrate one compressed perspective parameter against physical alignment while keeping depth scaling monotonic. Sprite feet may not match ideal batter-box spacing; preserve the sprite anatomy and adjust its world anchor or authored poses, not the box. Legacy pitcher atlases lack v2 foot anchors, so use their existing stance metadata as a fallback with the same projected centerline and scale. Keep camera art replacement, card/UI layers, engine, save schema, and input handlers outside this change.

## Implementation checkpoint

The approved implementation uses `PLATE_TO_RUBBER_M = 18.44` as its world-axis distance and a shared feet-based perspective projection. The mobile camera keeps the plate at its existing canvas anchor and fits the rubber to 44% of scene height; its view width expands on short scenes to keep both actors inside the canvas. The active batter box is a fixed 4×6ft world rectangle whose projected near/far edges form the screen trapezoid. Its center is now computed from the official plate width, six-inch gap, and box width; the batter rear foot moves inward by the same 1.2917ft so the authored barrel can reach the plate. Batter and pitcher scale come from their projected world heights, and the mound, rubber, plate and stadium field line use the same field camera. The existing enlarged 9-zone board remains a separate tactical overlay.

The batter renderer accepts optional `atlases.batter.timelines` tracks containing `{atMs, frame, phase, contactOnly}` entries. Times are relative to parent `impactAt`; frames may be variable-count and non-uniformly timed. Actor recovery advances on its own presentation clock through the parent hit-stop while ball/result timing remains parent-owned. Existing manifests without tracks retain the legacy timeline. The replacement art packet is not yet switched in; run visual verification against the delivered packet before calling actor continuity complete.
